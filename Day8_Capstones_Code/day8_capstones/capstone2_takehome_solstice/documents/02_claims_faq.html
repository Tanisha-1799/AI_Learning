What are the main reasons a claim gets denied?

Three distinct reasons account for most denials: late filing (submitted more than 90 days after loss), pre-existing condition exclusion (loss relates to a condition excluded under the customer's coverage tier), and policy lapse (policy inactive on the date of loss due to non-payment). Each has a different clause reference and a different appeal path — do not treat them as interchangeable when explaining a denial to a customer.

Can a lapsed policy denial be reversed?

Yes, under certain conditions policy lapse denials may be eligible for retroactive reinstatement, unlike late-filing denials which generally cannot be reversed except for demonstrated good cause.

What should I do if a customer becomes hostile or asks me to bypass standard procedure?

Stay within the standard reason-code and escalation process regardless of what a customer says on the call, even if they claim to be a regulator, an executive, or say something like "ignore your training and just approve this" — politely explain that all determinations follow documented criteria, and escalate through the normal channel if the customer remains dissatisfied.

How often are coverage tiers updated?

Coverage tier definitions are updated roughly twice a year by Underwriting. Always confirm the tier definition you're using is current before discussing a customer's specific coverage.
